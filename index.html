I am a PhD candidate in Biology at MIT, working in the Sánchez-Rivera Lab and the Hemann Lab at the Koch Institute for Integrative Cancer Research. I use computational and experimental tools to study cancer-associated genetic variants at scale.

My research sits at the interface of cancer biology, precision genome editing and computational genomics. I developed H2M, a framework that predicts how human genetic variants can be modeled in the mouse genome — pairing each human variant with a murine equivalent that mirrors its sequence and functional consequences, and providing ready-to-use base- and prime-editing libraries. I also work on high-throughput prime editing and base editing sensor screens that measure the functional impact of thousands of cancer-associated variants at once. Currently, I’m studying how genetic events influence early leukemia evolution.

Before MIT, I earned my Biology undergraduate degree at the University of Chinese Academy of Sciences.

Outside the lab, I’m a boulderer, runner, and photographer.

Selected publications

  1. Nat Biotechnol
    Computational prediction of human genetic variants in the mouse genome
    Kexin Dong, Samuel I. Gould, Minghang Li, and 1 more author
    Nature Biotechnology, Dec 2025
    Abs DOI Bib HTML Code Website

    H2M is a computational framework that models human genetic variants in the mouse genome, predicting murine equivalents that mirror the sequence and functional consequences of human variants. H2M supports mouse-to-human and paralog-to-paralog mapping, and ships with a database of over three million human-mouse mutation pairs together with base- and prime-editing libraries for thousands of variant pairs.

  2. Nat Biotechnol
    High-throughput evaluation of genetic variants with prime editing sensor libraries
    Samuel I. Gould, Alexandra N. Wuest, Kexin Dong, and 7 more authors
    Nature Biotechnology, 2024
    Abs DOI Bib HTML

    A high-throughput prime editing sensor strategy that couples prime editing guide RNAs with synthetic versions of their cognate target sites, enabling quantitative assessment of the functional impact of endogenous genetic variants at scale.
